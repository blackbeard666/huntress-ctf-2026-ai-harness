# ⚡ Recommended Gameplay

The core mechanic and differentiator of the Huntress CTF has always been: _**time**_. ⏰

If you’ve played the Huntress CTF in 2025 (_or 2024 or 2023!_), you are already familiar with the gimmick – for *a whole month*, new tasks release **_daily_**.

But with AI, _**time**_ is compressed... it is significantly easier now to do (mostly) anything in cybersecurity just by hitting the LLM slot machine. Frankly, this has just plain killed traditional Capture the Flag competitions… you just prompt enough times and eventually a flag pops out.

AI is an incredible feat of technology and it’s undeniable that our industry is adopting it everywhere – including us at [Huntress](https://huntress.com). So we wanted to lean in, embrace and _encourage_ the use of AI. 

While this is a strange thing to say, "I _don’t want **you** to play this CTF"…_ I want _your robot_ to play the CTF. 

AI has its faults though -- it's _non-deterministic_ and not as fast as strict syntax and machine code. On top of that, unless you're running local stuff, AI tokens cost real money! We want to minimize the Markdown and get to hard code 😎

So ***for you to get the most out of the 2026 Huntress CTF***, we suggest you **work ahead** of the timed daily releases. 💡

The idea behind this **AI Arena** concept is our challenge to you: can you build a **deterministic, general-purpose solver** for cybersecurity problems? Can you put a month-long CTF on autopilot? The premise is that you should arm yourself with tooling that lets you truly "*move at machine speed*" and handle whatever dynamic variation or entirely new challenge might be thrown at you.

## Food for Thought

- Can you automate everything so your agents can autonomously play the game every day?
- Can you enrich as much as possible with deep analysis at scale?
- Can your solutions survive different inputs, targets, environments, or variants?
- Where is the breaking point where you as a human still need to step in and manage the robot?
- What can you build to push that breaking point further away?
- How quickly can you solve an individual challenge? Can you make it faster? Is it as fast as possible?
- How quickly could you solve the *next* challenge if it uses the same underlying concept?
- Are you *"just"* solving the challenge, or are you creating a reusable capability?
- Can you expand on what you do today into more deterministic tooling for tomorrow?
- What would have to be true for your system to encounter a completely unfamiliar challenge and still know what to do next? 🤔

It's the "AI Arena", try to build your bot to beat the bots! 🤖
